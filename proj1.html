Project 1

Photo 1a Image 1
I love this photo of my 3 daughters and myself on vacation. However, the background is very complicated, and I wanted to emphasize all of us rather than the background.  I decided to create a layer mask to select pixels that I wanted to modify. To replicate this effect, follow the following instructions. 

Open your original image in Photoshop. 

First double click on the background layer to unlock it.  This will allow you to add modifications changing the image name to layer 0.

With your image layer 0 selected, at the top of the screen locate the Layer Menu, then select Duplicate Layer. This creates layer 0 copy that we can apply a layer mask to.

Since the names are similar, I renamed the top layer Photo Mask, by selecting the layer name in the Layers Panel and typing the new name. Then I changed the bottom layer name to Background.

With image layer Photo Mask selected, locate the toolbar on the left side of the interface. Select the Quick Selection tool (this is grouped with the Magic Wand tool and may be hidden). Next, use the Quick Selection tool to select all of the areas you want to modify. When you are selecting the area to modify, the brush menu at the top will show a + symbol. If you accidentally select too much area and need to remove it. Change the option to the - symbol in the menu to the top right. Do this until your selection is completed.

With your selection still enabled, locate the Layer Menu at the top left of the screen. Select Layer Menu, Layer Mask, then select Hide Selection (If you had selected the area you wanted to keep you would select Reveal Selection – which is the opposite masking effect).

In the layer panel, you will notice a new icon appeared on your Photo Mask layer. This indicates that the mask was applied.  Since you have an exact duplicate of your photo as your Background Layer, you will not notice a difference yet. 


To see the applied mask. In the Layers panel turn off the Layer Visibility button (looks like an eye on the far left side of the layer) on the Background Layer.

Now you can see the area that the mask is now hiding from your Photo Mask layer.

Turn the Layer Visibility button back on. 

Next double click on the Background Layer. This will open the Layer Style Menu. First, select the box labeled Bevel & Emboss. Next double click on Texture to bring up the Elements submenu (this also turns on the effect. In the Texture Elements submenu, select Pattern and select Cell (2nd from the right on the bottom). Change the Scale to 15 and the Depth to +34. Make sure the checkbox for Link with Layer is turned on. Then select OK to save your changes.

The picture looks more defined now, however I still want to darken the Background Layer. Again, double click on the Background Layer to open the Layer Style Menu. This time select the Color Overlay submenu.  With the Blend Mode at Normal set the color to a dark grey (Hex# 3e3b3b). Move the Opacity to 62.

Finally, on the left toolbar select the Horizontal Type Tool.  In the Text Menu above, select the Myriad Pro, Bold, 110 pixels, with a fill color of White (Hex#. Then click on the upper third of the screen and type “McManus Girl’s”.

To add a drop shadow to the text, double click the text layer in the Layer Menu. In the Layer Style Menu, select the Drop Shadow submenu to turn on the effect. Change the Angle to 135, the Distance to 13, Spread to 0, Size to 5 (leave all the other setting to their defaults).

Photo 1a Image 2
This is a picture of the Spokane Falls Bridge in Washington. The image has many wonderful features however it needs a little cleaning up. I removed the crane from the image. I adjusted the layers to increase the saturation in the image to brighten the composition.

To replicate this effect, follow the following instructions. 

Open your original image in Photoshop. 
First, double click on the Background layer to unlock it.  This will allow you to add modifications.

Now we need to duplicate the Layer 0, by pressing Ctrl-J(on a Mac – use Command-J). Double-click on the new Layer 0 copy to rename it Change Layer.
|
With your Change Layer selected, select the Quick Selection tool from the toolbar. In the top menu,  enable the Auto-Enhance box (this will create a smooth and accurate selection). Now draw a selection around the crane at the top of the image.  Make sure you leave the first lamp post (these will require you to remove the crane three times.

Expand the selection to include some background pixels by choosing the top menu Select, then Modify, then Expand and set the value to 10 pixels then hit OK to save the settings.

Now select the Edit menu and the Fill. Under Use select Content-Aware to save, click OK. Photoshop will fill in the selection with a group of the pixels surrounding the selection. You may need to change your selection and repeat this step until you have removed all the unwanted sections.

Next, I selected the Image menu, then Adjustments, then Levels. Under Channel change the setting to Red then adjust the left slider to the start of the black on the chart, I set this to 41. Now select Green and set the left slider to 41. Now select the Blue and set the left slider to 33. Avoid changing the RGB levels as one unit, since you lose the individual controls to adjust each color space in the image.

To boost the color in the image, I selected the Image menu, then Adjustments, then Hue Saturation. Change Master to Reds and increase the Saturation to +60. Change to Yellows and increase the Saturation to +22. Change to Greens and increase the Saturation to +37. Change to Cyans and increase the Saturation to +55. Change to Blues and increase the Saturation to +55. Change to Magentas and increase the Saturation to +44.

Finally, to enhance the image, we will replace the sky with a layer mask. With the Change layer selected, locate the toolbar on the left side of the interface. Select the Quick Selection tool (this is grouped with the Magic Wand tool and may be hidden). Next, use the Quick Selection tool to select all of the areas in the sky. Do this until the entire sky is selected.

With your selection still enabled, locate the Layer Menu at the top left of the screen. Select Layer Menu, Layer Mask, then select Hide Selection.
In the layer panel, you will notice a new icon appeared on your Photo Mask layer. This indicates that the mask was applied.  Since you have an exact duplicate of your photo under this layer, you will notice the crane has returned. 

Finally, to brighten our sky, insert a new layer with the File menu, then Place. Select the Cloud layer and size it by holding the Shift key while dragging one of the corners to the size needed to fill the top of the screen. Holding the Shift key allows you to maintain the aspect ratio of the image without it distorting the image. Hit Enter to accept the size transformation. Double click on the new layer and name it Cloud Layer.
Next, move the Cloud Layer under the Change Layer. Now you should have a dynamic photo that shows the Spokane Bridge in all its glory!

Project 1b Sound Editing
For this assignment, I edited the audio in GarageBand on my Macintosh Computer.
Using my friend as my voice talent, I recorded the audio clip. I then imported the audio into GarageBand. I adjusted the audio level of the Voiceover Layer to 2.7 decibels (dB). Since the voice over seemed way to fast and had no pauses, I separated most of the sentences by selecting the track and selecting Option T.

This created a break in the track where the cursor was. I then repeated this step 26 times adding gaps between the sound bites.

I then located the Acoustic Sunrise 1 loop track and added to the project as the Jingles layer. I shortened the length the at the end to (1min 6 sec) and set the level to -18.5 dB to bed the music under the voiceover. At the end of the music, I added a 1-second fade using keyframes.

I added three Sound Effect Tracks (SFX1-3) to enhance the depth of the audio presentation. Each SFX Layer had different dB values (SFX1 at -16.4 dB, SFX2 at
-21.5 dB, and SFX3 at -18.5 dB). I added sound the following effects tracks throughout the track.

1. Kids Cheering – 4sec (SFX2)
2. Arena Crowd Cheer 1 - 4 sec (SFX1)
3. Jumping in Pool – 8 sec (SFX2)
4. Clapping Crowd Studio 3 – 11 sec (SFX1)
5. Big Waterfall – 16 sec (SFX2)
6. Alarm Clock Bell -17 sec (SFX3)
7. Bark – 19 sec (SFX2)
8. Check Phone Ringing – 22 sec (SFX2)
9. Electronic Tune Up  – 32 sec (SFX2)
10. Stadium Crowd Chart 1  – 42 sec (SFX3)
11. Suspense Accents 6  – 1 min 2 sec (SFX1)

When I completed editing the audio, I saved the file to disk as an mp3 file. Then I uploaded the file to my Dropbox.com cloud storage. I then embedded the audio on this webpage for your listening pleasure.
